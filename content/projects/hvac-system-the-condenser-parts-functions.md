tagline: A five-minute HVAC micro-lesson that turns an unfamiliar backyard box
  into six parts a learner can name, explain, and safely power down.
summary: An instructional design work sample built around a single HVAC
  condenser unit most people walk past without understanding. The project
  includes a full narration script, an animated 17-slide storyboard, and
  supporting design documentation grounded in learning theory, from Gagné's Nine
  Events to Mayer's multimedia principles, built to take a learner from
  curiosity to a tested, safety-critical skill in under five minutes.
problem: "New HVAC learners -and everyday building occupants- can recognize a
  condenser unit without understanding what's inside it, or, more critically,
  how to power it down safely before maintenance. Existing technical
  documentation tends to be dense, jargon-heavy, and disconnected from where a
  learner's confusion actually starts: the plain metal box sitting outside a
  building. This project set out to close that gap for someone with zero prior
  HVAC background, ending with a specific, testable safety skill rather than
  general familiarity."
approach: >-
  I started with backward design: defining a single three-part learning
  objective (identify, describe, locate) before writing a word of script or
  sketching a single slide. The lesson structure follows Gagné's Nine Events of
  Instruction, moving from a curiosity hook through six components taught in
  true physical cause-and-effect order, into a safety-critical procedure
  reinforced with deliberate spaced repetition, and closing with a self-graded,
  retrieval-based practice exercise.


  Every visual and animation choice, the coil's color-coded heat gradient, highlight timing synced to narration, a single "system map" diagram reused from introduction through recap through practice,  was made against a specific principle from Cognitive Load Theory or Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning, not aesthetic preference. Deliverables include a full narration script, a 17-slide storyboard, a slide-by-slide design rationale explaining every visual decision, and a companion document detailing the instructional theories applied throughout.
outcome: "This project was completed as a self-directed instructional design
  work sample, comprising four deliverables: a narration script, an animated
  storyboard, a slide-by-slide design rationale, and a theoretical framework
  document. It demonstrates a complete, defensible instructional design process,
  from objective-first planning through learning-theory-grounded execution, and
  is production-ready for voiceover recording and animation."
