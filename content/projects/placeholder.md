tagline: Lead with head and heart — the EQ skills every educational leader needs
  when the stakes (and emotions) run high.
summary: This microlearning course explores emotional intelligence as a core
  leadership competency for principals, department chairs, deans, and other
  educational administrators. Grounded in the research of Salovey, Mayer, and
  Goleman, the course walks through the five components of EQ — self-awareness,
  self-regulation, motivation, empathy, and social skills — using realistic
  school leadership scenarios (tense faculty meetings, difficult conversations,
  policy rollouts). Along the way, learners engage with research insights (e.g.,
  Gross's work on cognitive reappraisal, Edmondson & Lei's research on
  psychological safety), practical frameworks like the SBI
  (Situation-Behavior-Impact) feedback model, and tools like a personal EQ
  self-assessment and a Monthly 360° Check-in. The course closes with reflective
  journaling prompts and guidance for treating EQ development as an ongoing
  practice rather than a one-time skill.
problem: Educational leaders are routinely thrust into emotionally charged
  situations — anxious staff, conflict-laden meetings, high-stakes policy
  changes — often without any formal training in managing their own emotions or
  reading the emotional needs of the people they lead. This gap shows up as
  avoided difficult conversations, eroded staff trust, low psychological safety,
  and burnout. Traditional leadership training tends to focus on strategy and
  operations while treating "people skills" as a soft, secondary concern — even
  though research shows a leader's day-to-day emotional behavior is one of the
  strongest predictors of team trust, learning, and performance. This course
  reframes EQ as a core, learnable professional competency rather than an innate
  trait, giving leaders concrete tools instead of vague advice.
outcome: >-
  Define emotional intelligence and explain why it predicts leadership
  effectiveness as strongly as (or more than) traditional intelligence

  Distinguish the five components of EQ (self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills) and recognize each in day-to-day leadership situations

  Apply cognitive reappraisal techniques to regulate emotions under pressure, rather than defaulting to suppression

  Practice deep listening as a concrete empathy skill in one-on-one and group settings

  Identify the relational levers of influence (reciprocity, credibility, vision, connection) that build trust with faculty and staff

  Deliver clear, low-defensiveness feedback using the SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact) model

  Recognize the leader behaviors that build (or erode) psychological safety on a team

  Use a personal EQ self-assessment and a Monthly 360° Check-in process to track growth over time
