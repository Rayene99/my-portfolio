summary: >-
  Designing onboarding training for teachers who are new to both online delivery
  and advanced adult learners taught me something: you can't just hand people a
  framework. You have to make them feel it.


  I built "Beyond the Language: Applying Bloom's Taxonomy" an onboarding module (one of many in the training) for novice teachers stepping into online classrooms with advanced adult (B1-B2+) students.


  The goal wasn't to explain Bloom's Taxonomy in the abstract. It was to show teachers how to move learners from lower to higher order thinking skills,  and how to redesign their teaching so English becomes a tool for critical thinking, not just a subject to master.


  I made few decisions behind the design:


  🎯 Minimal by intent: I was the live facilitator, so the slides carried only key concepts and visuals. I avoided walls of text because I wanted the full attention to be directed towards my explanation and I took into consideration that  inexperienced teachers may feel overwhelmed by crowded slides easily.


  🧩 Interactive over passive: Clickable hotspots, reveal states, and tabbed navigation let trainees explore each Bloom's level (like "Create: produce new or original work") at their own pace instead of just watching.


  🛠️ Built in Adobe Captivate: Used it to combine animation, branching interactions, and responsive layout for a genuinely engaging async/live hybrid experience.


  🎓 Grounded in Andragogy: Every design choice assumed adult learners: autonomy, relevance, and application over rote instruction.


  Projects like this are why I love the intersection of instructional design and onboarding; turning a theoretical framework into something a room full of new trainers can actually use anytime.
